How Liam Education Eliminated Sheet Reconciliation For Good

A business English training center moved learner records, classes and tuition from multiple Google Sheets into a CRM and LMS sharing one profile.

Deployment profile

Industry
Business English training
Learners
Working professionals, not school or university students
Products deployed
CRMLMS
Previous system
Google Sheets per department

Liam Education connects the learner journey from sign-up to classroom

The CRM and LMS share one learner profile, so the team spends less time reconciling by hand and works from one data source.

01

A center that teaches one thing, to one group of people

Liam Education teaches English for the workplace. Its learners are working professionals who sign up to speak confidently in meetings, emails and conversations with partners, so the center doesn't follow the exam-prep model for students that most of the market does.

This learner group brings an operational quirk: many come through corporate contracts, when companies send staff to study in batches. One consultation can lead to many learners, and schedules have to fit around their working hours.

02

When reconciling data becomes a job of its own

Learner records, consultation schedules, class progress and tuition receipts were scattered across many Google Spreadsheets, with each department keeping its own. None was wrong, but none fully matched the others.

To answer basic questions like which learners had paid, which classes had taken attendance, or which corporate contracts were awaiting confirmation, the center had to hire part-timers and outsource just to match the sheets against each other. That's payroll spent on work nobody should need to do.

Meanwhile, full-time staff were consulting, teaching and handling admin all at once. That repetitive workload drove turnover up, and every time someone left, the learner care history in their personal files and memory left with them.

03

CRM for the learner journey, LMS for the classroom

The center rolled out both products at once, with clearly split scopes so they don't overlap. The CRM manages everything before a learner joins a class, and the LMS manages everything after.

CRM

Before class

Track learners from the moment they're a lead requesting a consultation to enrollment, including the channel where corporate clients send staff in batches.

LMS

After class starts

Place learners by workplace communication level, take attendance each session, assign and grade work, and track each learner's progress.

One shared profile so consultants and instructors see the same data, instead of each side keeping its own version.

A normal working day, before and after

The biggest change isn't having more software; it's that steps that used to be done by hand simply no longer exist.

On many spreadsheets

Before
  • Instructors took attendance in class, then re-entered it into a spreadsheet after class ended.
  • Consultants had to open several files to find out whether a learner had paid and which class they were in.
  • Corporate contracts lived in loose notes kept by whoever handled each batch.

On one system

After
  • Instructors take attendance right in class, with no re-entering into a spreadsheet afterwards.
  • Consultants open a learner profile and see the full conversation history, tuition status and current class on one screen.
  • Corporate contracts are tracked by each batch of staff sent to study, right on the contract record.

Three changes the center has seen

No more reconciliation staff

With learner, class and tuition data in one system, there's no longer any reason to match spreadsheets against each other.

Less pressure on the team

Repetitive admin work has been cut, so full-time staff spend their time consulting and teaching, removing a direct cause of turnover.

Handovers don't depend on former staff

New consultants take over a profile and can read its full history immediately, instead of starting over with existing customers.

Why this chain of problems repeats across businesses

Liam Education isn't an isolated case. Any business that lets data scatter by department goes through the same chain; only the file names differ.

Unified data

Customer information, transactions and documents live on one platform instead of many spreadsheets kept by each department.

Less reliance on temporary staff

No more hiring extra people just to cross-check figures between spreadsheets.

Less pressure driving turnover

Cutting repetitive manual work is the most direct way to keep the staff you have.

Easy handovers

Work history lives in the system, so new people get up to speed fast without former staff walking them through it.

Decisions on current numbers

Reports come from live data, not last week's manual summary.
